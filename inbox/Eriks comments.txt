Erik, your comment made me go back through the curator logic. Much of what you describe is already present, just not yet tightly connected. ARPL’s work classes define a simulation/preview before commit and a specific “proof of done.” Level 4 project workflows add deliverable intent, production sequence, user decisions, discipline authority and recorded results. There is also the beginning of the unit-testing mechanism you describe. Packs can contain executable final/checks/, but the curator only accepts them if they are deterministic and read-only. Usage reports record the pack + revision, environment/agent, pass/fail and checks run. Successful reproduction in another model, Revit version or bridge becomes evidence toward moving a rule from candidate → reproduced → verified. Where I think your point lands is the next step: connecting these pieces into a formal acceptance contract for the deliverable — expected state, tolerances, invariants, required checks and pass/fail criteria — with an execution report. Then the same workflow can be replayed against different model/agent versions and drift becomes measurable rather than anecdotal. So yes “unit testing for deliverables” is a useful framing. That distinction is useful.
